# Bridal OS — Demo & Discovery Script

> **The honest one-liner:** *"A calmer bride journey from sale to pickup — a
> simple, beautiful page where each bride sees fittings, balances, documents,
> and what's next. It works alongside whatever you use to run the sale."*

⚠️ **Important competitive reality:** **BridalLive already has a Client Portal**
(orders, payments, measurements, lookbook, event management, timeline). So do
**not** claim "BridalLive has no bride portal" or "we're the bride-facing layer
BridalLive lacks." That's false and a power user will call it out instantly.

This is **discovery first, demo second.** Your job is to find out whether the
pain is real, not to push the product. The strongest asset is still the
no-login bride journey page — but only show it once you've earned the right.

---

## The one question that decides the whole call

Open with this, before any pitch:

> **"Are you using BridalLive Client Portal today?"**

Then branch:

| Their answer | What it means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| **"No / not really / never set it up"** | Best target | Discovery → demo (below) |
| **"Yes, but brides still text/call about X"** | Premium "concierge" wedge | Dig into the leak, then demo against *that* gap |
| **"Yes, and it works great"** | Not your customer | Thank them, ask for a referral, move on |

If they're a happy Client Portal power user, **say so and bow out.** That
honesty is what earns trust (and referrals) with the rest.

---

## Discovery questions (ask before demoing)

1. "Do you use BridalLive — and is the **Client Portal** turned on?"
2. "After a bride buys the dress, what does she actually **see**?"
3. "Where do **fittings, balances, alteration updates, and documents** live?"
4. "What still comes in by **text or call** that you wish didn't?"
5. "What does your team **forget or chase** between the sale and pickup?"
6. "Would a simple bride journey page **help**, or just **duplicate** what you've
   got?"

You're listening for one of:
> *"We have BridalLive but never turned the portal on."*
> *"We use the portal but brides still text us about fittings/alterations."*
> *"We're not on BridalLive at all — this would be perfect."*

If you don't hear one of those, **stop. Don't demo. Reposition.**

---

## The framing line (only after discovery confirms a gap)

> "This isn't a replacement for BridalLive, your POS, or your register — keep
> all of that. It's a lightweight page for the part of the bride journey that
> still lives in texts and sticky notes after the dress is sold. Let me show
> you the bride's view."

## The sample bride story

- **Sienna Martinez**, wedding **~6 months out**.
- Dress: **$3,500 total, $1,500 deposit paid**, $2,000 balance.
- Needs: a **first fitting**, a **hem alteration**, a **final balance payment**,
  and her **signed contract** on file.

(Mirrors the built-in sample data, so it's safe to demo live.)

## The 5-minute demo flow

### 1. Add the bride (45 sec)
- **Add new bride** → name, wedding date, total ($3,500), paid ($1,500).
- **Say:** "Thirty seconds of entry. Your POS still owns the actual order — this
  is just her journey page."

### 2. Build her timeline (90 sec)
- **Dress tab:** designer, style, size, color.
- **Appointments tab:** add a **First Fitting** next week (note the live
  conflict check).
- **Alterations tab:** **"Hem 2 inches – Maria,"** $150.
- **Documents tab:** upload the contract PDF, toggle **"Show to bride."**

### 3. Send the page (30 sec)
- Click **Portal link** → copy. "No password, no app to download."

### 4. THE MOMENT — open the bride page on a phone (90 sec)
- Turn the screen to them. Walk through what *she* sees: countdown, journey
  timeline, next appointment (she can **request** a time), **confirm
  measurements**, **payment schedule + Pay Now**, **documents**.
- **Say:** "This is the part owners react to — it's calm, it's branded as you,
  and it cuts the 'when's my fitting / what do I owe' texts."

### 5. Close the loop (45 sec)
- Show the **appointment-request inbox** and **balances** view on the dashboard.

## What to say vs. what NOT to claim

### Say (all true today)
- "Works **alongside** your POS — BridalLive, Square, Shopify, Lightspeed, etc."
- "A simple, **no-login** journey page for each bride."
- "Fittings, alterations, balances, reminders, and documents in one place."
- "Automated email reminders; online payment via Stripe."
- "Set up in an afternoon."

### Do NOT claim
- ❌ **"BridalLive has no bride portal" / "the layer BridalLive lacks."** False —
  BridalLive has a Client Portal. Never imply otherwise.
- ❌ Any **specific metric** (98% engagement, 60% less admin, 32% fewer
  no-shows). Only cite a number if a real pilot gave it to you.
- ❌ **Multi-location, team roles, custom workflows** — roadmap, not built.
- ❌ **Importing from / syncing with BridalLive** — there is **no** integration.
  Manual or CSV entry only. Say so plainly.
- ❌ **SMS**, **SOC2/certifications**, inventory/accounting/POS features.

## Objection handling

**"Doesn't BridalLive already do this?"**
> "Their Client Portal does a lot — if you've got it fully rolled out and brides
> love it, you may not need us, honestly. Where I tend to help is the stuff that
> *still* happens outside it: alteration updates, balance nudges, documents, the
> reassurance texts. Is any of that still landing on your team?"

**"We're not on BridalLive."**
> "Even better — you don't need a full bridal operating system to give every
> bride a clear 'here's what's next' page. That's all this is."

**"How is this different from a CRM?"**
> "A generic CRM doesn't know what a fitting or an alteration is, and it won't
> give your bride a page. This is built for the months between yes-to-the-dress
> and pickup."
